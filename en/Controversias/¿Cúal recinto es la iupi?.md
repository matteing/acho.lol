## The debate

Students at **UPR Río Piedras** insist that *la Iupi* refers exclusively to their campus. They will call out—sometimes aggressively—students from any other campus who use the term for the whole UPR system.

Students from **other campuses** such as Mayagüez, Bayamón, and Cayey sometimes use *la Iupi* for the complete system.

## The arguments

### For Río Piedras only

- The term originated at Río Piedras.
- Every campus has its own nickname; Mayagüez is *El Colegio*.
- The name expresses campus identity and pride.
- *La Iupi* carries cultural associations specific to Río Piedras: activism, bohemian life, and the humanities.

### For general use

- Every campus belongs to the UPR system.
- Saying *la Iupi* is easier than specifying a campus.
- A nickname should not be gatekept.

## Usage

- *¿Tú estudiaste en la Iupi? — Sí, en Río Piedras.* — Did you study at *la Iupi*? Yes, at Río Piedras.
- *Yo también estudié en la Iupi. — ¿En qué recinto? — En Mayagüez.* — I studied at *la Iupi* too. Which campus? Mayagüez.
- *Eso es El Colegio, webón, no la Iupi.* — That is *El Colegio*, dumbass, not *la Iupi*.
