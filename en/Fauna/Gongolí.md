## What is it?

**Gongolí** is a Puerto Rican name for several kinds of millipede: the long, segmented little animals with an extraordinary number of legs that appear in damp places. People casually call them insects, but they are technically **diplopods**, a different class of arthropod.

![[../../Fauna/_attachments/gongoli-enrollado.png|A red gongolí curled up on wood]]

The name does not identify one species. Puerto Rico’s Department of Natural and Environmental Resources documents several local *gongolíes*, including the giant *gongolo* (*Orthocricus arboreus*), leaf-litter *gongolí* (*Phinocricus parcus*), and red *gongolí* (*Trigoniulus lumbricinus*).

## How to recognize one

- Its body is long, cylindrical, and divided into many segments.
- Most segments carry **two pairs of legs**, which gives the class *Diplopoda* its scientific name.
- It usually lives under leaves, rocks, wood, and other cool, damp hiding places.
- When threatened, it curls into a spiral: the classic “I am not available right now” of the Puerto Rican yard.

## What it does

Millipedes help decompose leaves and other plant matter. They do not need to win the lottery; they already live among food, shade, and moisture.

Many curl up in self-defense, and some species release strong-smelling or irritating substances. Observe without squeezing them, and wash your hands after handling one.

## It is not a centipede

A *gongolí* moves slowly, has a generally cylindrical body, and carries two pairs of legs on most segments. A centipede is usually flatter and has one pair per segment. They may look related, but they are not the same creature.

## Names and regional variants

Depending on the town and family, you may hear **gongolí**, **gongoló**, **gongolón**, **gungulén**, or simply **milpiés**. Puerto Rico’s 100 × 35 miles somehow make room for a whole *reguero* of names.
