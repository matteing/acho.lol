An exclamation of frustration, anger, surprise, joy, or admiration—basically useful for everything.

Depending on the audience, it may sound vulgar or genuinely disturbing. Religious or superstitious listeners in particular may object:

> *Nene, no digas eso ni de chiste.* — Kid, do not say that even as a joke.

In a Puerto Rican accent, the sounds compress and shift into the phonetic expression **marrayoparta**.

## Closest English equivalents

“Damn it” or “fuck,” but with archaeological layers of Catholic euphemism on top.

The original curse, *mal rayo te parta*, literally wishes that a bad bolt of lightning would strike and split **someone else** apart.

## Usage

- *Me rayo parta, ¿qué pasó aquí?* — Damn, what happened here?
- *¡Marrayoparta! Se me cayó el café.* — Fuck! I dropped my coffee.
- *Me rayo parta con este tráfico.* — Damn this traffic.

## Variants

- *Mal rayo te parta* — the original full curse.
- *Me rayo parta* — an intermediate phonetic reshaping.
- *Marrayoparta* — the fully compressed slang spelling. It is not standard written Spanish, but it is authentically Puerto Rican and appears in texts, memes, and tweets exactly as it sounds.

## Origin

The original *mal rayo te parta* is a curse directed at another person, wishing for lightning to strike and split them in two. Many Puerto Ricans say forms such as *marrayo palta* without realizing the underlying phrase. Over time its sounds compressed into *marrayoparta*, which now works as a general-purpose exclamation.
