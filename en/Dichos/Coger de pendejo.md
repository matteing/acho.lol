## Meaning

To be deceived, exploited, or treated like an idiot. It also describes someone trying to make a fool of you.

## Closest English equivalents

“To get played,” “to be taken for a fool,” or “to treat someone like an idiot.”

## Usage

- *Me cogió de pendejo con ese negocio.* — They played me in that deal.
- *A mí nadie me coge de pendejo.* — Nobody makes a fool of me.
- *No te dejes coger de pendejo.* — Do not let them take you for a fool.

## Variants

- *Coger de pendejo/a.*
- *Dejarse coger de pendejo.*
- *No me cojas de pendejo.*

## Origin and nuance

The phrase combines *coger* (“to take” or “grab” in Puerto Rico) with *pendejo* (“fool” or “idiot”). It imagines someone handling you as though you were stupid.

Related expressions include *tomarle el pelo* (to pull someone’s leg), *hacerle un mamey* (to trick someone), and *raquetear* (to scam someone).
