## Meaning

An expression for someone acting like a clown—trying too hard to be funny, making a fool of themselves, or carrying the joke too far. It references **Tatín**, Puerto Rico’s most famous clown.

![[../../_attachments/tatin-payaso.png]]

**Tatín** (Luis Rafael Sánchez Salgado) is one of Puerto Rico’s most beloved clowns. For decades he entertained generations of children on television and at live events, and he remains a respected figure in Puerto Rican popular culture.

## Closest English equivalents

“He’s being a clown” or “What, did you eat a clown for breakfast?” The Puerto Rican phrase is funnier because *Tatín* is a specific shared cultural reference.

## Usage

- *Mira a ése, se comió un Tatín.* — Look at that guy; he must have eaten Tatín for breakfast.

## Variants

- *Se comió un Tatín.*
- *¿Te comiste un Tatín?*
- *Parece que se comió un Tatín.*
