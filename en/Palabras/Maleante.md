A criminal, gangster, or person who lives by the street—from the rawest meaning, someone who carries a weapon and sells drugs.

The word can also mock any fool who buys an absurd gun and starts imagining himself as the toughest man in the street. It may be insulting, descriptive, or—even respectfully used—describe a “real” *maleante* who follows [[en/Palabras/Códigos|the code]], stays quiet, and keeps his word.

## Closest English equivalents

- “Thug,” “gangster,” or “criminal.”
- In music, “gangsta.”

## Usage

![[../../Palabras/_attachments/maleante-anuel-meme.jpg]]

> *Se cree maleante pero no hace nada.* — He thinks he is a gangster but never does anything.

> *Escucha Hades y se cree el más maliante el welebicho.* — The motherfucker listens to Hades and thinks he is the biggest gangster around.

## *Malianteo*

*Malianteo* turns the life of a *maleante* into a noun: the lifestyle, the culture, and above all a musical style.

## Variants

- *Maliante / maleante* — singular person.
- *Maleantes* — plural.
- *Maleanteo* — the lifestyle or musical genre.
- *Malianteo* — a common online spelling.

## Origin and cultural context

*Maleante* is not exclusive to Puerto Rico; it appears throughout the Caribbean and Latin America. Puerto Rico gave it a distinct cultural turn by tying it to underground reggaetón and trap. *Música de maleanteo*, with stark lyrics about public housing and street life, defined an entire era of the genre.
