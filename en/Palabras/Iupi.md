## Meaning

A nickname for the **University of Puerto Rico, Río Piedras Campus**—official home of the [[en/Palabras/Pelús|*pelús de la Iupi*]], said here with affection.

Some people use *la Iupi* for the entire UPR system, but Río Piedras students strongly reject that usage.

> [!WARNING] The naming debate
> Río Piedras students insist that *la Iupi* means Río Piedras only. Other campuses have their own nicknames: UPR Mayagüez, for example, is **El Colegio**.
>
> See [[en/Controversias/¿Cúal recinto es la iupi?|Which campus is *la Iupi*?]].

## Closest English equivalent

There is no translation; it is a proper-name nickname.

## Usage

- *Yo estudié en la Iupi.* — I studied at UPR Río Piedras.
- *Hoy hay huelga en la Iupi.* — There is a strike at *la Iupi* today.
- *La Iupi tiene la mejor facultad de humanidades.* — *La Iupi* has the best humanities faculty.

## Origin

The name probably comes from pronouncing the first two letters of UPR in English: “U-P” → “you-pee” → *Iupi*. Honestly, we still have [[en/Dichos/Ni puta idea|*ni puta idea*]] exactly where it came from.

UPR Río Piedras is a major center of student activism, art, and the humanities. The nickname carries campus pride, political engagement, and a distinctive bohemian identity, in contrast with the engineering and technical reputation of *El Colegio*.

## Political connotations

Río Piedras students have historically been associated with the left, social activism, and pro-independence or socialist positions. That reputation grew through decades of student strikes, protests against the United States military presence—including Vieques—and resistance to cuts in public-education funding.

Depending on the speaker, calling someone an *estudiante de la Iupi* may imply:

- **Positively:** educated, socially committed, politically aware.
- **Negatively:** radical, “communist,” disorderly, or always wasting time on strikes.

Puerto Rican music and politics sometimes invoke *la Iupi* as shorthand for educated leftists or *izquierdosos*, often loosely and dismissively. Tempo used the campus this way during his *tiraera* with Residente, presenting university education as pretentious and contrasting it with street authority.
