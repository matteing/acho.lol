*Cabrón* is an essential Puerto Rican expression. It can mean an astonishing number of things: sometimes insulting, sometimes affectionate.

If you were not raised in Puerto Rico, it is easy to misunderstand *cabrón* in a sentence—or to use it badly. Tone is everything. Younger people especially use it as an intensifier or an affectionate name among friends. Older people and formal settings may find it offensive or vulgar. Use it at your own risk, dear gringito. Puerto Rico is not called *[[en/Palabras/Calentón|el calentón]]* for nothing.

## Meaning

An extremely versatile word whose meaning depends on context, tone, and the relationship between speakers. It can be an insult, an affectionate form of address, an intensifier, or an adjective.

### 1. Insult

An asshole, bastard, or motherfucker. It communicates anger or condemnation.

> *Ese wlb es un cabrón.* — That motherfucker is an asshole.

### 2. Affectionate address

Among friends, it can mean “dude,” “bro,” or “man,” delivered playfully or warmly.

> *Dímelo, cabrón, ¿qué es la que hay?* — What’s up, man? What’s going on?

### 3. Intensifier or exclamation

It emphasizes surprise, frustration, or admiration without necessarily insulting anyone.

> *Diablo, cabrón, tengo ganas de una [[en/Palabras/Combi|combi china]]… ¿nos tiramos?* — Damn, dude, I’m craving a Chinese combo. Should we go?

### 4. Adjective: clever or formidable

It can describe someone sharp, resourceful, impressive, or always able to get their way.

## Closest English equivalents

- “Asshole” or “bastard” as an insult.
- “Dude,” “bro,” or “man” affectionately.
- “Damn” or “man” as an exclamation.
- “Clever,” “shrewd,” or “badass” as an adjective.

## Variants

- *Cabrón* — masculine singular.
- *Cabrona* — feminine singular; insulting or playful.
- *Cabrones* — plural, any gender.
- *Cabroncito* — diminutive and generally more playful.

## Important nuances

- **Tone:** Friendly versus aggressive changes everything.
- **Relationship:** Playful among friends; offensive among strangers.
- **Context:** What happened before the word affects its meaning.
- **Intensity:** A soft *cabrón* and a shouted *¡CABRÓN!* carry very different weight.
