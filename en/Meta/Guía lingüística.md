This is a living guide for maintaining a consistent Boricua voice without erasing real variation in Puerto Rican speech.

## Principles

- **Write as Puerto Ricans speak when a spelling is established.** For terms, examples, and informal voice, prefer recognizable Boricua forms.
- **Do not write everything phonetically.** Explanatory prose may use standard Spanish. We do not remove every *d*, *s*, or *r* simply because speakers sometimes do.
- **Do not “correct” quotations.** Direct quotations, lyrics, and proper names retain the source spelling. Explain variants outside the quotation when needed.
- **Document variation.** When several forms coexist, describe their context instead of declaring one universal throughout Puerto Rico.
- **Avoid invented dialect.** A contraction should reflect real usage, not serve as decoration that makes writing merely “sound more Boricua.”

## Preferred forms

| Use | Avoid by default | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **tiraera / tiraeras** | *tiradera / tiraderas* | Loss of the *d* is established in this context. Keep the form with *d* in quotations or when discussing that variant itself. |

This table grows as new editorial questions appear. Never mechanically generalize one decision to every similar-looking word.

## Translation principle

The English edition teaches culture; it does not try to replace Puerto Rican Spanish. Keep terms, names, quotations, lyrics, and culturally specific references in their original language. Translate or gloss only what helps readers understand meaning, register, context, and the joke.
